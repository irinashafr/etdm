What this module is about

5 ECTS125 hours total36 in class · 89 your ownExam: team presentation

Most people can name a business model. Very few can say where a company actually captures the money, as opposed to where it says it creates value — and the gap between those two things is where digital businesses live and die.

This module has two halves that turn out to be one. First: how entrepreneurs decide when nothing can be predicted, which is a learnable logic and not a personality type. Second: how digital models actually work — network effects, subsidised sides, near-zero marginal cost, and the awkward arithmetic underneath the pitch deck.

You will take real companies apart, find the single assumption holding each one up, and build a transformation plan for a business that has not yet made the jump. You will also, repeatedly, be asked what your own thinking is doing to your judgement.

What you need to know in week one

  • You register one company in Session 4 and work on it for the rest of the semester. One company per team, first registered keeps it.
  • You work in a fixed team of five from Session 1 to Session 12.
  • You keep four pieces of paper. They are dated, you make them in class, and you hand them in at the end. Lost sheets are not reissued.
  • The exam is a team presentation in Session 11 or 12. Seven minutes, then questions — and every member answers at least one.

AI-OFFLive and unaided—

The live defence in Sessions 11 and 12 is AI-OFF. You have known that since week one.

A model will write you a fluent means inventory in four seconds and every word of it will be false, because it does not know whom you know.

Assume AI is in the room. The skill this module builds is catching it being confidently wrong about things you can check — which is the whole of Session 8.
