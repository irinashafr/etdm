What this module is about

5 ECTS125 hours total36 in class · 89 your ownExam: poster

Most people can name a business model. Very few can say where a company actually captures the money, as opposed to where it says it creates value — and the gap between those two things is where digital businesses live and die.

This module has two halves that turn out to be one. First: how entrepreneurs decide when nothing can be predicted, which is a learnable logic and not a personality type. Second: how digital models actually work — network effects, subsidised sides, near-zero marginal cost, and the awkward arithmetic underneath the pitch deck.

You will take a real company apart, find the single assumption holding it up, and build a transformation plan for it. From week four you will also be building a venture of your own, in that company's market, and finding out what it takes to attack it.

What you need to know in week one

  • You register one company in Session 4 and work on it for the rest of the semester. One company per team, first registered keeps it.
  • You work in a fixed team of five from Session 1 to Session 12.
  • From Session 4 you run Founder Mode — nine episodes in which you build a venture in your registered company's market and defend your decisions against four AI classmates.
  • You keep everything you write. Dated, made in class, handed in at the end. Lost sheets are not reissued.
  • The exam is a poster. Your team mounts it; you are then questioned at it, individually, for two minutes.

A poster has no room to hide. No company history, no agenda, no team photograph, no more than 400 words of prose. What survives the compression is the evidence and the decision — which is exactly what the two minutes of questions are about.

Founder Mode

An interactive simulation that runs alongside the course from Session 4. You develop a venture through nine connected episodes: making decisions under uncertainty, investigating markets and competitors, testing assumptions, working with business models and unit economics, and responding to evidence as it arrives.

Your venture launches into your registered firm's market. You are the challenger. That is deliberate — by week eleven you will know that incumbent's weak points in a way no desk research produces, and that is what your poster and your defence rest on.

Four persistent AI classmates challenge what you decide, from customer, financial, strategic and execution perspectives. They remember what you decided last time.

They are not markers and they are not oracles. They are right often enough to be useful and wrong often enough to be worth auditing — and Episode 5 requires you to catch one of them being confidently wrong about your market, with a contradicting source and a date.

By week eleven you will have made roughly forty decisions in this market. The examiner will ask you one question about a number. You will have been asked worse, weekly, by something that does not get tired.
